# Singapore attractions and neighbourhoods

Singapore combines a compact city centre with distinctive cultural districts, waterfront areas, gardens and family attractions. Marina Bay is appropriate for first-time visitors interested in the skyline, Gardens by the Bay, the waterfront promenade and nearby museums. Gardens by the Bay contains both outdoor gardens and climate-controlled conservatories, so a visit can be adapted when rain or heat makes outdoor plans uncomfortable.

Chinatown is useful for heritage streets, places of worship, traditional businesses and food. Kampong Glam, centred around Arab Street and Haji Lane, offers Malay-Arab heritage, the Sultan Mosque area, textiles, cafes and street art. Little India offers colourful streets, temples, markets and Indian food. These three areas work well for a cultural itinerary, but visitors should dress respectfully when entering religious sites.

Sentosa is a leisure island with beaches and ticketed family attractions. Singapore Zoo, River Wonders and Night Safari form a wildlife cluster outside the central area and require meaningful travel time. The Singapore Botanic Gardens is a major outdoor green space; its National Orchid Garden is a focused paid attraction inside the larger gardens.

Useful indoor or weather-resistant choices include the National Museum of Singapore, Asian Civilisations Museum, ArtScience Museum, Jewel Changi Airport, the Cloud Forest and Flower Dome conservatories, and major shopping or dining complexes. Opening hours and ticket availability can change, so travellers should verify them with the official attraction before visiting.
