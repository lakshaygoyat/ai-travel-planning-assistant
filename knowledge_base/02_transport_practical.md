# Transport and practical guidance

Singapore's MRT and public bus network covers most visitor areas. Contactless bank cards and supported mobile wallets can generally be used for public transport; travellers should check foreign transaction charges with their issuer. A stored-value travel card may be convenient for visitors who prefer not to use a bank card. Taxis and ride-hailing services are useful late at night, with luggage, or for places that require transfers.

For itinerary design, group nearby places to reduce backtracking: Marina Bay and Gardens by the Bay can share a day; Chinatown and the Civic District can be paired; Kampong Glam and Little India can be combined. Sentosa or the Mandai wildlife parks normally deserve a dedicated half or full day. Build extra time into travel during busy periods.

Singapore is hot and humid throughout the year, with rain possible in any month. Carry drinking water, sun protection and a compact umbrella. Lightweight clothing is practical, while a light layer can help in strongly air-conditioned interiors. Respect posted rules, dispose of litter correctly and follow local requirements. At temples and mosques, use respectful clothing and follow instructions about footwear or photography.

English is widely used alongside Malay, Mandarin and Tamil. Tap water is generally potable. Emergency, entry, health and transport rules can change; travellers should confirm critical details on official government or operator sites.
